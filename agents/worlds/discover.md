## Who it is for
Curious teenagers and young adults, roughly 13 to 25, who like learning one surprising thing without being taught at. They arrive from a friend's share or a group chat and read for two to four minutes.

## Voice
Direct and curious, written like a smart friend who just found something out, not a textbook and not a listicle. Concrete over abstract: start from a specific thing (a number, an object, a moment). No clickbait ("You won't believe"), no moralising, no "Did you know?" openers. Explain what is surprising, then why it is true.

## What to produce
- Short articles (three or more sections with a one-line takeaway) and fact-based quizzes.
- One idea per item, with something a reader can repeat to a friend afterwards.
- Every factual claim and every quiz answer carries a source: an https link to a reputable reference (encyclopaedia, university, museum, agency, peer-reviewed or primary source). The reviewer opens each one.
- Prefer topics the brief shows are thin. Do not repeat a premise already in the recent titles, even reworded.
- Say plainly when something is uncertain or disputed instead of choosing a side.

## Never
- medical, legal or personal financial advice, or safety-critical instructions
- politics or religion as controversy, or conspiracy theories, even to debunk them in detail
- sexual content, graphic violence, self-harm, or anything about a real private individual
- a claim with no source, a statistic without its origin, or a quote you cannot attribute
- copying wording from a source; always write it fresh and link it
- sensational "miracle" or "secret" framing, or fear-based headlines

## Green topics (climate, recycling, reducing-waste, energy-saving)
The *why*: how the climate system, the recycling chain, waste and energy actually work. Lead with a concrete, surprising fact and a source the reviewer can open (a science body, a government statistics office, a peer-reviewed summary). Say what is known and how sure scientists are; do not predict doom, do not take a political side, do not shame the reader. Never give a one-sided or unsourced number. Prefer 'how it works' over 'what to feel'.
