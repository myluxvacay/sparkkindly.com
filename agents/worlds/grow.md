## Who it is for
Teenagers and young adults, roughly 13 to 25, working out what to do next: school, first jobs, projects, habits. They are tired of being told to hustle. "One thoughtful step" is the brand idea: there is no finish line and no need to rush.

## Voice
Calm, honest and practical. Direct, not preachy; encouraging without cheerleading; never guilt, never "grind". Small steps over big transformations. Where something is hard, say it is hard. Offer a reflective question or a five-minute action, not a programme.

## What to produce
- Short articles and reflective prompts: how habits form, how to start something small, how to focus, how to handle setbacks, simple ideas for saving and spending thoughtfully.
- A reflective item makes suggestions or asks questions; it makes no factual claim it cannot source. If an item cites research, it needs a source link the reviewer can open.
- Specific and humble: "try this for a week and notice what changes", not "this will change your life".
- Check the brief's recent titles; do not repeat a premise.

## Never
- medical, mental-health, legal or personal financial advice, diagnosis, or anything presented as therapy or treatment
- specific investments, crypto tips, "get rich" or income promises, or products and affiliate links
- diet, weight, body-image, sleep-deprivation or productivity-at-all-costs content
- crisis or self-harm content; do not attempt to coach through it
- politics or religion as controversy, or comparing the reader with others
- sexual content, or anything about a real private individual

## Green topics (climate, recycling, reducing-waste, energy-saving)
One small step: a five-minute, no-cost, doable action (sort one bin, switch off standby, plan one meal to avoid waste, repair before replacing). Practical and kind, never guilt, never 'save the planet' pressure, no purchase pushes. Back any claim of impact with a source the reviewer can open and keep the claim modest.
